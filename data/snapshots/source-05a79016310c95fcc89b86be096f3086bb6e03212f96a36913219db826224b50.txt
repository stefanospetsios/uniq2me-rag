Executive summary


 
   
Security fused into the network.


 
   
Enterprise networks are undergoing a fundamental transformation. Organizations must securely connect an expanding ecosystem of users, devices, applications, cloud services, AI-driven workloads, and autonomous agents while maintaining operational simplicity, resilience, and security. Hybrid workforces, distributed campuses and branches, cloud-native applications, operational technology, IoT devices, and emerging AI services have dramatically increased the scale and complexity of modern environments.


 
   
At the same time, the threat landscape continues to evolve. AI-powered attacks now operate at machine speed, data exfiltration remains a top business risk, and traditional perimeter-focused security models are no longer sufficient. Organizations must not only secure external access points but also continuously authenticate users, devices, and agents, enforce least-privilege access, contain lateral movement, and maintain visibility across increasingly dynamic environments.


 
   
These challenges require a new approach where networking and security operate as a unified system rather than separate technology domains. Cisco Secure Networking (CSN) fuses security into the fabric of the enterprise network, making the network an ever-present security layer that protects customer data, financial systems, business continuity, regulatory compliance, and digital experiences wherever the business runs. As the traditional perimeter dissolves, fragmented, bolt-on security cannot keep pace with machine-speed exploitation, AI-driven lateral movement, infrastructure-targeted attacks, compromised identities, and the “harvest-now, decrypt-later” quantum threat. 


 
   
By strengthening infrastructure resilience, establishing common policy context through Cisco Data Fabric, and using unified management and agentic operations, CSN gives networking and security teams a connected foundation for protecting infrastructure, traffic, workloads, people, AI agents, and digital experiences.


 
   
Delivering value via three key pillars


 
   
As enterprise environments continue to evolve, the network must deliver three connected outcomes. First, organizations need a trusted, resilient infrastructure. Second, the network must protect every flow. Finally, Unified Management and Agentic Operations must bring these capabilities together through assurance, automation, and action for people and AI agents.


 
   
Cisco Secure Networking delivers these outcomes through three pillars: 


 
   
Trust the Infrastructure
: Cisco strengthens infrastructure resilience across campus, branch, WAN, remote access, cloud, and workload environments. This secure infrastructure connects what is allowed, enforces policy, and provides the telemetry and common policy context needed for distributed enforcement and assurance.


 
   
●     
Establish Device Trust by verifying every platform from power-on through runtime using technologies such as secure boot, hardened operating system implementations, image signing, runtime integrity monitoring, and Cisco Live Protect.


 
   
●     
Act as the intelligent foundation for connectivity, distributed policy enforcement, and telemetry, enabling operational services and a common policy context.


 
   
●     
Prove trust through modern advanced crypto agility with technologies such as MACsec, post-quantum readiness, and continuous compliance evidence.


 
   
Secure Every Flow
: The network must protect every interaction across users, devices, workloads, applications, and AI agents. This involves securing all steps across the infrastructure from entry through application and data access. Cisco Data Fabric carries the data, telemetry, and context required to apply policy consistently across domains, while distributed enforcement capabilities enact those policies. Threat intelligence and analytics then enable detection and response at machine speed to reduce threat impact and accelerate recovery.


 
   
Cisco Secure Networking protects every flow through four connected functions:


 
   
●     
Secure Infrastructure
: The trusted, resilient foundation consisting of the physical topology, routed Layer 3 underlay, OSPF transport, device reachability, MTU consistency, redundancy, and foundational telemetry.


 
   
●     
Identify and Authorize:
 Identity- and context-aware access control using Cisco ISE, 802.1X, MAB, endpoint profiling, authorization profiles, and Security Group Tag assignment.


 
   
●     
Segment and Protect:
 Least-privilege reachability control using VLANs, VRFs, VXLAN EVPN, SGTs, TrustSec, Adaptive Policy, SD-WAN segmentation, and distributed firewall enforcement.


 
   
●     
Detect and Respond:
 Detection, correlation, and governed containment using network telemetry, encrypted traffic analytics, firewall inspection, threat intelligence, XDR, and distributed response actions.


 
   
Assure Every Experience
: Unified Management and Agentic Operations, delivered through Cisco Cloud Control, unify the platform through one operating model. Assurance, automation, and governed action help networking and security teams preserve availability, performance, and the digital experience while maintaining human control.


 
   
Cisco Cloud Control brings these capabilities together through:


 
   
·
        
Centralized Management connects inventory, topology, health, assurance, security, and operational context through Cisco Cloud Control and Cisco Data Fabric.


 
   
·
        
Agentic Operations turn shared context and operational insight into governed action at machine speed while maintaining human control.


 
   
·
        
Continuous Assurance by verifying security, availability, performance, and policy outcomes end to end.


 
   
Together, these pillars address the three pressures above. The next section explains why they must work as one architecture.


 
   
Challenge statement


 
   
Why now? Threats and operations move at machine speed.


 
   
The enterprise attack surface is evolving faster than legacy security models can respond. AI-accelerated exploitation is shrinking vulnerability-remediation windows and increasing the risk to network devices and control planes, while “harvest-now, decrypt-later” attacks threaten long-lived data and expose the lack of crypto agility in existing infrastructure. At the same time, AI agents are emerging as high-privilege identities whose access, traffic, and behavior can be difficult to discover and govern, creating new paths for lateral movement. Fragmented tools, overlapping policies, and limited end-to-end visibility prevent NetOps and SecOps from acting quickly without disrupting connectivity or the digital experience. Organizations need a more integrated approach that can keep infrastructure resilient, govern every identity, protect every flow, and assure every experience. 


 
   
Three pressures expose legacy security limits


 
   
1.
    
Infrastructure risk is outpacing traditional protection.
 AI can compress vulnerability-remediation windows, target network devices and control planes, and increase exposure before teams can patch. Long-lived data also faces harvest-now, decrypt-later risk when infrastructure lacks crypto agility.


 
   
2.
    
Every new identity expands the attack surface.
 AI agents can hold credentials, access sensitive applications and data, and generate traffic that existing tools cannot always discover or govern. They must be identified, continuously verified, governed, and contained with the same rigor applied to human and machine identities.


 
   
3.
    
Visibility gaps put every experience at risk.
 Fragmented NetOps and SecOps tools, overlapping policies, and limited end-to-end visibility make it difficult to understand policy impact or act quickly without disrupting legitimate traffic and the digital experience.


 
   
The three pillars of Cisco Secure Networking provide a structured response to these press
ures.

Why fragmented controls reach a limit


 
   
Point products remain viable within their domains, but enterprise outcomes depend on how identity, policy, detection, response, and assurance connect across domains; whether identity context reaches enforcement, segmentation follows identity rather than topology, detection triggers coordinated responses, and assurance verifies that the resulting action protects both security and user experience. A collection of controls is not yet an architecture.

Mapping functions to a Reference Architecture


 
   
Mapping the three pillars and four security functions to the architectural framework.


 
   
To design a network that delivers the desired business outcomes, platform capabilities must map to the three pillars within a coherent architectural model. The design begins with Secure Infrastructure; uses Common Policy Context to identify and authorize, segment and protect, detect, and respond; and connects these functions through Unified Management and Agentic Operations with Extended Integrations.

CSN architectural layers and functions


 
   
This is Secure Infrastructure.


 
   
This is the foundation of the architectural model. Secure Infrastructure provides secure access, resilient connectivity, distributed enforcement, foundational telemetry, and network services that support the identity, segmentation, detection, response, and operational capabilities above it.


 
   
Trust the Infrastructure means more than hardening the device. Cisco establishes and sustains trust from power-on through runtime with features such as secure boot, signed images, runtime integrity, and Cisco LiveProtect, helping ensure that the device itself is uncompromised. Cisco Data Fabric brings data, telemetry, and context together to establish a Common Policy Context so every policy engine can use the same facts and the intelligent network can apply and enforce policy in a distributed manner.


 
   
This reference architecture defines the common design logic and interactions for Cisco Secure Networking. Deployment guides are available for specific deployment scenarios and provide product-, topology-, and release-specific implementation guidance. Teams can use the applicable deployment guide to translate the architecture into a deployable design.


 
   
The four functions of securing every flow


 
   
The four functions form a closed-loop control sequence. They are most powerful when they share identity, policy, and telemetry, but they do not have to wait for a single management plane or a complete platform migration to begin delivering value. In the architectural framework, the functions sit between Secure Infrastructure and Unified Management and Agentic Operations. Common Policy Context allows each function to use the same facts, while Extended Integrations carry policy, telemetry, and governed action beyond the core architecture.


 
   
1.
    
Identify and Authorize
: Continuously discover, profile, and classify users, devices, workloads, applications, services, and nonhuman identities. Assess posture and context, then grant the minimum access required. Authorization becomes a living decision rather than a one-time login event.


 
   
2.
    
Segment and Protect
: Translate identity and business intent into segmentation that limits unnecessary communication and is carried throughout the infrastructure. Enforce policy close to the resource across campus, branch, cloud, data center, and workload environments. Layer network, firewall, and workload controls so a single control failure does not enable unrestricted lateral movement.


 
   
3.
    
Detect
. Use pervasive network telemetry, encrypted traffic analytics, firewall inspection, DNS signals, workload behavior, and threat intelligence to surface anomalous communications. The goal is to reduce blind spots while preserving privacy and operational feasibility.


 
   
4.
    
Respond
. Correlate evidence, recommend or orchestrate the appropriate action, and contain threats close to the affected identity, endpoint, segment, workload, or flow. Response should be governed, reversible, and designed to preserve essential service.


 
   


 
   
Unified Management and Agentic Operations with Cisco Cloud Control


 
   
Cisco Cloud Control unifies inventory, topology, health, assurance, security, and operational context through one operating model. Its agentic capabilities accelerate investigation and recommend bounded actions for people and AI agents, with guardrails, auditability, approvals, and human control. Continuous assurance then verifies security efficacy, connectivity, performance, and the resulting digital experience.


 
   
Extended Integrations


 
   
Extended Integrations carry policy, telemetry, shared context, threat intelligence, and governed automation beyond the core Cisco architecture. They connect IT service management, partner SIEM and SOC platforms, supported third-party firewalls, identity providers, APIs, infrastructure as code, cloud platforms, collaboration, IoT, and AI data center environments.

Resilient connectivity, routing, availability, and failover


 
   
●     
Scalable macrosegmentation and microsegmentation


 
   
●     
Identity-driven access, authorization, and posture validation


 
   
●     
Application, remote access, WAN, and cloud connectivity


 
   
●     
Policy enforcement across network and security domains


 
   
●     
Telemetry, observability, centralized management, and assurance


 
   
●     
Operational workflows, including onboarding, policy changes, and service disruptions


 
   
●     
Integrations with security, analytics, observability, and IT service-management platforms


 
   
Testing considered both steady-state operations and dynamic or degraded conditions to assess end-to-end architectural behavior.

What validation demonstrated


 
   
Validation demonstrates that the Secure Network Reference Architecture can deliver consistent operational and security outcomes across its architecture domains when deployed according to validated design guidance. The architecture supports resilient connectivity, scalable segmentation, identity-driven policy enforcement, and consistent access across wired, wireless, remote access, and application environments.


 
   
Unified Management and Agentic Operations provide lifecycle management, shared telemetry, policy orchestration, and operational assurance. Extended Integrations connect security, analytics, observability, and IT service-management platforms to support coordinated workflows and improved operational response.


 
   
Together, these capabilities support scalable growth, resilient operations, predictable performance, consistent policy enforcement, and operational efficiency across supported deployment models.


 
   
A proof-of-value method


 
   
1.
    
Baseline the current state, including operational effort, exposure, reachability, detection and containment time, application experience, and change failure rate.


 
   
2.
    
Select one high-value outcome and design representative normal, degraded, and adversarial scenarios.


 
   
3.
    
Execute using the intended deployment model, identity sources, traffic paths, policy, and integrations.


 
   
4.
    
Observe whether context is correlated across identity, network, security, and experience telemetry.


 
   
5.
    
Contain through the intended enforcement point and verify that essential service remains available.


 
   
6.
    
Recover or roll back, document evidence, and compare results with the baseline before scaling.


 
   
Representative scenarios


 
   
1.
    
A compromised valid identity attempts lateral movement.


 
   
2.
    
An unmanaged or industrial device initiates prohibited communications.


 
   
3.
    
Suspicious behavior is hidden inside encrypted traffic.


 
   
4.
    
A critical infrastructure exposure must be mitigated before a patch window.


 
   
5.
    
Secure agentic AI:
 An AI agent with valid credentials attempts to invoke an unapproved tool or access data outside its task scope. Identify the agent and owner, apply least privilege, inspect agent-to-tool traffic, detect abnormal behavior, and require bounded, auditable action with human control.

Implement framework incrementally


 
   
A practical operating model.


 
   
Secure networking does not require a single cutover or a universal management plane. The framework can guide staged modernization around the customer’s highest-risk flows and most valuable outcomes. The platform approach represents the target state; the sequence below provides early evidence while building reusable identity, policy, telemetry, and automation foundations.


 
   
1. Align on outcomes and ownership


 
   
Bring NetOps, SecOps, identity, cloud, application, industrial, and service-management leaders into one decision model. Define the first outcome, the accountable owner, the risk boundary, and the measures that will determine success.


 
   
2. Establish trusted context


 
   
Inventory critical infrastructure and flows. Improve identity and asset discovery. Normalize role, posture, workload, and application context. Identify where policy translations or stale privileges create avoidable exposure.


 
   
3. Reduce reachability and improve detection


 
   
Apply segmentation to a bounded use case, add telemetry at the relevant paths, and connect detection to response. Favor controls that preserve business communication while removing unnecessary paths.


 
   
4. Automate with governance


 
   
Automate stable, repeatable actions first. Define approval thresholds, service-impact guardrails, logging, exception handling, and rollback before expanding to agentic workflows or higher-impact change.


 
   
5. Assure, learn, and scale


 
   
Verify user and application experience after every control change. Use evidence from incidents, policy exceptions, and proof-of-value testing to refine the architecture and prioritize the next domain.


 
   


 
   
Summary: Why Cisco


 
   
One architecture across network, security, and experience.


 
   
Secure networking is needed because enterprise risk now travels through the same distributed relationships that deliver the business. Users, devices, workloads, applications, APIs, and AI agents must communicate across infrastructure the enterprise does not fully own. Protecting those interactions requires more than visibility and more than a collection of perimeter controls. It requires a trusted, identity-aware, distributed, closed-loop architecture. 


 
   
Cisco’s differentiated value


 
   
1.
    
The network becomes an enforcement fabric.
 Cisco can apply identity-aware policy close to users, devices, workloads, and resources across campus, branch, data center, cloud, and remote access.


 
   
2.
    
Security and networking share context.
 Identity, policy, telemetry, threat intelligence, and experience data can inform the same control loop instead of remaining trapped in separate operational domains.


 
   
3.
    
Trust begins below the policy layer.
 Hardware and software integrity, runtime protection, encryption, exposure prioritization, and cryptographic readiness protect the infrastructure that carries and enforces intent.


 
   
4.
    
Response is distributed and experience-aware.
 Controls can contain closer to the affected flow while assurance verifies that essential service and digital experience remain intact.


 
   
5.
    
Governed Agentic Operations turns connected context into action.
 AI-assisted investigation and bounded automation can accelerate response while guardrails, auditability, approvals, rollback, and organizational policy keep people in control.


 
   
6.
    
The architecture accommodates choice and staged adoption.
 Open integrations and multiple deployment models let customers extend current investments while converging on shared functions and measurable outcomes.


 
   
Cloud Control unifies the operating model, Data Fabric provides shared context, and resilient infrastructure provides the foundation for protecting flows and experiences.
