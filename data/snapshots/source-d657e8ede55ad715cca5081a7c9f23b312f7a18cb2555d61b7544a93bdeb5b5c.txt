I. Foreword
With DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act), Regulation (EU) 2022/2554 on digital
operational resilience for the financial sector, the European Union has established cross-
financial sector European regulation in the areas of digital operational resilience, ICT risks and
cybersecurity. This regulation entered into force on 16 January 2023 and will be applied from
17 January 2025. All financial entities must comply with the DORA requirements from this
date onwards.

ICT risk management is at the heart of DORA. It is intended to provide financial entities with a
framework enabling them to systematically identify, assess and manage their ICT risks. In
principle, the ICT risk management requirements therefore relate to the same issues which
the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht –
BaFin) addresses through its supervisory requirements for IT (Supervisory Requirements for IT
in Financial Institutions (Bankaufsichtliche Anforderungen an die IT – BAIT)/Supervisory
Requirements for IT in Insurance Undertakings (Versicherungsaufsichtliche Anforderungen an
die IT – VAIT/Supervisory Requirements for IT in Asset Management Companies
(Kapitalverwaltungsaufsichtliche Anforderungen an die IT – KAIT)/Supervisory Requirements
for IT in Payment Services and Electronic Money Institutions (Zahlungsdiensteaufsichtliche
Anforderungen an die IT von Zahlungs- und E-Geld-Instituten – ZAIT)). Nonetheless, there are
methodological differences in the approaches pursued in these sets of regulations; this may
pose challenges with regard to compliance with the DORA requirements.

This supervisory statement is intended to help financial entities implement the DORA
requirements for ICT risk management (Chapter II) and ICT third-party risk management
(Section I of Chapter V), including the relevant regulatory technical standards (RTS) 1. In
particular, it is intended for entities supervised by BaFin which fall under the scope of the
BAIT or the VAIT and will in future be required to comply, inter alia, with the ICT risk
management requirements set out in Articles 5 to 15 of DORA. While these guidance notes
only relate to the BAIT/VAIT, the supervisory requirements for asset management companies
and for payment services and electronic money institutions (KAIT/ZAIT), which are not
explicitly considered here, are similar in many cases. The guidance notes are thus generally
relevant in these fields as well.

These guidance notes on implementation are based on the findings of six working groups
which were set up in 2023 and consisted of representatives of the financial industry, the
Deutsche Bundesbank and BaFin. These working groups compared the requirements of
DORA regarding the above-mentioned “standard ICT risk management framework” and the
“key principles for sound management of ICT third-party risk” (Art. 28-30 of DORA) as well as
the related draft RTS with the requirements of chapters 1 to 10 of the BAIT and VAIT in order
to identify significant changes and any resulting need for action 2. The other chapters of
DORA were not considered in this context (cf. Figure 1).

II. Guidance notes on implementation
The following guidance notes on the implementation of the DORA requirements consist of
the following sections: governance and organisation; information risk and information
security management; IT operations; ICT business continuity management; IT project
management and application development; ICT third-party risk management; operational
information security; and identity and rights management.

Overall, the BAIT and VAIT requirements largely match DORA’s requirements with regard to
the “standard ICT risk management framework” (Art. 5-15 of DORA) and the “key principles
for sound management of ICT third-party risk” (Art. 28-30 of DORA) as well as the relevant
Level 2 legal texts or their drafts. In addition, due to its focus on digital operational resilience ,
DORA includes requirements which are currently not present in the BAIT/VAIT.

In light of this assessment and of DORA’s transposition in Germany through the German
Financial Market Digitalisation Act (Finanzmarktdigitalisierungsgesetz – FinmadiG), BaFin
intends to withdraw its supervisory requirements for IT (BAIT/VAIT/KAIT/ZAIT). In the case of
financial entities which do not fall under the scope of DORA, it should be noted that
measures to appropriately deal with IT/cyber risks are in any event required as part of a
proper business organisation.



1. Governance and organisation

This section compares the requirements of Art. 5 and 6 of DORA and Art. 2(2) of RTS RMF
relating to the governance and organisation of ICT risk management in DORA with those
stipulated in the IT strategy (chapter 1) and IT governance (chapter 2) chapters of the
BAIT/VAIT.

DORA does not specify an IT strategy as it is required in chapter 1 of the BAIT/VAIT. Instead,
DORA includes requirements relating to a digital operational resilience strategy (hereinafter:
“DOR strategy”). Since these two strategies cover different aspects, the DOR strategy
requirements are in some cases new.

DORA focuses on the governance and organisation of ICT risk management, i.e. the effective
and prudent management of ICT risk, in order to strengthen individual financial entities’ level
of digital resilience by means of an internal governance and control framework. On the other
hand, information security and related governance requirements are at the heart of the
BAIT/VAIT requirements. The differences are also apparent in terms of the contents of the
new DOR strategy as a central strategy for the ICT risk management framework, as well as the
management body’s expanded tasks.

Moreover, Art. 6(8)(g) of DORA refers to extensive additional requirements relating to digital
operational resilience testing (cf. Chapter IV, Art. 24-27 of DORA). The findings of the tests
described in these requirements are intended, inter alia, to mitigate ICT risks and attain
specific ICT objectives. However, these guidance notes on implementation do not cover these
additional requirements from Chapter IV.

1.1 DORA requires new strategy for digital operational readiness

DORA introduces a new DOR strategy (Art. 6(8) of DORA). This strategy focuses on ICT risk
management, which also includes ICT third-party risk management. By way of comparison,
the BAIT/VAIT set out IT strategy requirements, i.e. a functional and cross-cutting overall IT
strategy which is considerably broader. These differences are also evident in the minimum
requirements for the contents of the two strategies, meaning that the two strategies cannot
be equated. Certain contents which the BAIT/VAIT require for an IT strategy are also included
in DORA as part of its general, non-strategy-related requirements. These generally form part
of its ICT governance requirements. For instance, the strategic development of the
organisational and operational structure of IT (chapter 1.2 (a) of the BAIT/VAIT) has been
incorporated in DORA as the ICT organisational requirements below the strategic level
(Art. 5(2) and Art. 6(5) of DORA). The DOR strategy does not include any explicit comments
regarding IT business continuity management such as are listed in the IT strategy in the
BAIT/VAIT. However, the topic of ICT business continuity management is a requirement which
is set out in the ICT risk management framework (see section 4) and is thus included in the
management body's tasks (see section 1.3).

Nor does the DOR strategy prescribe the implementation of the information securi ty
requirements stipulated in the IT strategy in the context of established standards
(chapter 1.2 (b) of the BAIT/VAIT). DORA does, however, considerably less prominently
propose the consideration of established standards (Art. 2(2)(h) of RTS RMF) and likewise
remains neutral with regard to standards in terms of its requirements and their
implementation by financial entities.

In terms of the objectives, responsibilities and the integration of information security in the
organisational structure (chapter 1.2 (c) of the BAIT/VAIT), the DOR strategy contains
corresponding requirements. The DOR strategy defines clear, verifiable information security
objectives here. The ICT third-party risk strategy under Art. 28(2) of DORA and the optional
strategy for the use of multiple ICT third-party providers set out in Art. 6(9) of DORA must be
considered in relation to connections with ICT third-party risks. On the other hand, the
embedding of information security in the organisational units is reflected in DORA at the
level of the respective guidelines for ICT security.

The strategic development of the IT architecture (chapter 1.2 (d) of the BAIT/VAIT) is
comparable with the requirements of the DOR strategy in Art. 6(8) of DORA regarding the ICT
reference architecture, incl. information on any changes which may be required for the
attainment of specific business objectives. However, DORA introduces a new term “ICT
reference architecture” which is not defined in Art. 3 of DORA, leading to possible difficulty in
distinguishing between these two terms.

Unlike the VAIT, DORA does not make any stipulation regarding the form of the document –
whether it is drawn up separately or as part of the financial entity’s business strategy.
However, the assumption that strategies may be combined as appropriate continues to
apply. The topic of appropriate communication of an IT strategy (chapter 1.5 of the VAIT) is
not covered in DORA. However, general sectoral governance requirements must be taken
into consideration here.

While DORA does not require an IT strategy as in the BAIT/VAIT, the continued existence of
such a strategy is certainly necessary and sensible: both as a potential link between the
business strategy and the DOR strategy and against the background of sectoral governance
requirements for strategies.



1.2 Comprehensive, but ICT-specific internal governance and control framework

The internal ICT governance and control framework is a core area of focus in DORA. It should
be noted that the general, non-ICT-specific governance requirements set out in the sectoral
provisions continue to apply. DORA addresses the governance of the ICT risk management
framework, i.e. the effective and prudent management of ICT risks and, as a result,
strengthening the resilience of the respective financial entity. The focus is somewhat different
in the BAIT/VAIT and relates, in particular, to information security and the associated
governance requirements.

DORA also requires an overall risk perspective, since ICT risk management is included in
financial entities’ general risk management (Art. 6(1) of DORA). Like the BAIT/VAIT (cf.
preliminary comments in chapter 4.2 of the VAIT, chapter 4.2 of the BAIT/VAIT), in this
respect DORA emphasises the ultimate and overall responsibility of the management body
(Art. 5(2)(a), (d) of DORA).

With regard to the establishment of an ICT risk control function with responsibility for the
management and oversight of ICT risk pursuant to Art. 6(4) of DORA (cf. section 2.2), DORA
requires appropriate segregation and independence in relation to the various functions. In
general, the BAIT/VAIT require the avoidance of conflicts of interest and incompatible
activities within the organisational and operational structure of IT (chapter 2.4/2.7 of the
BAIT/VAIT). DORA requires the three lines of defence model or the use of other internal
models for risk management and control (Art. 6(4) of DORA).

As well as the ICT risk control function, Art. 5(3) of DORA prescribes the establishment of a
monitoring function which is to monitor contracts with ICT third-party service providers for
the use of ICT services. This can either be performed by a role which is to be established for
this purpose or else by a member of senior management (see also section 6.6).

The general requirements for ICT security policies prescribed in Art. 2(2) of RTS RMF are not
found in the BAIT/VAIT. However, there are certainly content and contextual overlaps
between the individual ICT security policies and the BAIT/VAIT. Financial entities may need to
make changes on account of the differences.

DORA likewise considers technological advancements and the current and future threat level
(chapter 2.3/2.4 of the BAIT/VAIT), although it uses different terms here (cf. inter alia recital
no. 48 of DORA and Art. 2(2) of RTS RMF).



1.3 Significant expansion of the management body’s tasks

With detailed requirements and tasks, DORA assigns considerably greater responsibility to
the management body (Art. 5(2) of DORA) and thus strengthens its role in the context of

governance and organisation. At specific points, the BAIT/VAIT contain similar requirements
as a task for the management board (such as IT strategy, provisions regarding the
organisational and operational structure of IT, management of IT operations, IRM,
information security policy, ISM, investigation of information security incidents, IT project
management), but they are less extensive.

Art. 5(4) of DORA requires members of the management body to have sufficient knowledge
and skills regarding the ICT risks which are to be managed and also to actively keep the skills
and knowledge up to date. There is no explicit equivalent requirement in the BAIT/VAIT;
these circulars instead require, in general terms, appropriate quantitative and qualitative
resources.

In many cases, the management body of the financial entity is required to define, approve,
oversee and assume responsibility for safeguards in connection with the ICT risk
management framework (cf. in particular Art. 6(1) of DORA).

       Under Art. 5(2)(b) of DORA, policies must be introduced in order to maintain high
        standards in relation to DORA’s four protection objectives. These include the
        information security policy set out in Art. 9(4)(a) of DORA, the ICT business continuity
        policy and the ICT third-party risk management policy.
       The ICT security policies listed in RTS RMF must likewise be approved by the
        management body (Art. 2(2)(b) of RTS RMF). The BAIT/VAIT do not include any similar
        requirement at the level of policies.
       The avoidance of conflicts of interest by means of suitable organisational measures,
        which is prescribed in chapter 2.4/2.7 of the BAIT/VAIT, also features in DORA's
        requirements. While DORA does not explicitly mention the “avoidance of conflicts of
        interest”, this follows from the governance requirements and is subsequently
        specified, in particular, in Art. 2(2)(g) of RTS RMF. Under Art. 5(2)(c) of DORA, the
        management body is responsible for setting clear tasks and responsibilities for all ICT-
        related functions.
       The implementation of the financial entity’s ICT business continuity policy and ICT
        response and recovery plans must be periodically approved, overseen and reviewed.
       As with the BAIT/VAIT, DORA requires appropriate resources in order to comply with
        the digital operational resilience requirements; this includes the ICT skills for all staff.
        The management body is responsible for ensuring this (Art. 5(2)(g) of DORA).
       The internal ICT audit plans of the ICT audit department and significant changes to
        these plans must be periodically approved and reviewed.



2. Information risk and information security management

This section compares the requirements for information risk and information security
management as well as the ICT risk management framework in Art. 3, 5, 6, 8, 13, 14, 45 and
49 of DORA and Art. 3-5 and 27 of RTS RMF with those in the information risk management
and information security management chapters of the BAIT/VAIT.

ICT risk management is of key significance for DORA in order to attain the objective of digital
operational resilience. In comparison with the BAIT/VAIT, DORA places considerably greater
emphasis on ICT risk management than information security. This is also evident in terms of

Guidance notes on the implementation of DORA                                            Page 10 of 39
the introduction of the ICT risk control function, which is to assume “responsibility for
managing and overseeing ICT risk“. This resembles the information security officer (ISO) in
the BAIT/VAIT. However, the ISO has greater responsibility for information security matters.

In addition, DORA introduces new check and analysis requirements regarding ICT risks, new
technologies, legacy systems, incidents and tests and related reporting obligations, including
in relation to the supervisory authority. For instance, DORA stipulates an at least annual or
else event-driven review of the ICT risk management framework. The supervisory authority
may require this to be provided in the form of a report. Furthermore, DORA emphasises
training obligations and communication strategies in the context of digital operational
resilience.



2.1 Shift in emphasis from information security to ICT risk management

The significantly greater importance placed on ICT risk management over information
security represents a fundamental shift in emphasis. According to DORA, ICT risk
management serves as the basis for safeguarding digital operational resilience, and
information security measures are to be used for this purpose. The BAIT/VAIT focus on
information security measures and the risk assessment is of secondary significance. However,
the measures actually required in practice in either case are not likely to differ significantly.
Nevertheless, a stronger focus on the ICT risk on the basis of this shift in emphasis, with its
corresponding prioritisation at the level of implementation, is certainly conceivable.

Nor does DORA stipulate any ban on outsourcing ICT risk management functions. Art. 6(10)
of DORA explicitly refers to the possibility of outsourcing “tasks of verifying compliance with
ICT risk management requirements”. However, in the event of outsourcing, sectoral
requirements3 must still be complied with in regard to outsourceability. In a specific
outsourcing scenario, these sectoral provisions may certainly define requirements, conditions
or limits for such outsourcing. In addition, the financial entity remains fully responsible for
safeguarding ICT risk management.

The definition of an information domain in accordance with chapter 3.3/3.4 of the BAIT/VAIT
and the method for determination of the protection requirements in accordance with
chapter 3.4/3.5 of the BAIT/VAIT are replaced by ICT asset management and classification of
these assets. DORA refers here to the determination or identification and classification of ICT -
based business functions as well as information assets and ICT assets which support these
functions (Art. 8(1) of DORA and Art. 4 and 5 of RTS RMF). Critical assets and dependencies
on ICT third-party service providers and risks resulting from cyber threats and ICT
vulnerabilities must be identified. These are to be documented in inventories. The obligation
to specify the configuration of information assets and ICT assets and the
connections/interdependencies between these assets means that this resembles a
configuration management database (CMDB). If coverage of DORA’s specific requirements is

2.2 Stronger focus on analytical and control activities

Art. 6(4) of DORA introduces an ICT risk control function which closely resembles the
information security officer (ISO) required in chapter 4.4/4.5 of the BAIT/VAIT in terms of its
status and independence. However, the former is not identical to the latter. The ISO is to be
responsible for “all information security issues”, while the control function is to assume
“responsibility for managing and overseeing ICT risk”. The transformation of the ISO into an
ICT risk control function seems appropriate in order to reflect this change. If the goal is for
the ISO to assume the tasks of the ICT risk control function which is to be set up, financial
entities should ensure that the ISO likewise assumes the ICT risk management tasks. The less
detailed information provided regarding the concrete tasks of the ICT risk control function
also establishes scope for a revised set of tasks.

DORA prescribes extensive new check and analysis requirements regarding ICT risks, new
technologies, legacy systems, incidents and tests as well as related reporting obligations, in
some cases also in relation to the supervisory authority. These include in particular:

       A review of the ICT risk management framework under Art. 6(5) of DORA is to be
        implemented at least once a year or on an event-driven basis upon the occurrence of
        major ICT-related incidents and in case of conclusions derived from testing
        (Chapter IV of DORA) or audits. This serves to continuously improve the ICT risk
        management framework. The supervisory authority may require a review and request
        a report on the review (Title II, Chapter V of RTS RMF).
       Following major ICT-related incidents which disrupt core activities, the cause of the
        disruption must be analysed and required improvements identified (Art. 13(2) of
        DORA). The promptness in responding, any forensic analyses, escalation and
        communication must also be covered.
       The specific risks for all legacy ICT systems must be assessed at least annually
        (Art. 8(7) in conjunction with Art. 3(3) of DORA). This risk assessment must be
        conducted “in any case before and after connecting technologies, applications or
        systems”.
       Senior ICT staff are to report to the management body at least annually on findings
        resulting from incidents and tests and put forward their related recommendations
        (Art. 13(5) of DORA). In particular, this concerns cyber-attacks and other contingency-
        related incidents as well as threat-led penetration testing (TLPT) and supervisory
        reviews.

It can be assumed that new processes must be established or existing ones revised in order
to comply with these analytical and reporting obligations. In addition to the retrospective
review of ICT-related incidents and past performance of the ICT risk management framework,
new technological developments are also to be monitored, including in relation to cyber-
attacks (Art. 13(7) DORA and Art. 3(1)(e) of RTS RMF). The effects arising from the use of new
technologies for ICT security and digital operational resilience are to be examined here. A

core area of focus is the monitoring of the “latest” ICT risk management processes in order to
effectively combat new and existing forms of cyber-attacks.



2.3 Strengthening of training and communication

DORA places a considerably stronger emphasis than the BAIT/VAIT on training obligations.
Financial entities are thus required to develop ICT security awareness programmes and digital
operational resilience training for all employees and senior management staff (Art. 13(6) of
DORA). In addition, the members of the management body are required to keep up -to-date
with their knowledge and skills relating to ICT risks, including by means of specific training
(Art. 5(4) of DORA). In general, this training is to be commensurate to the remit of their
functions and also cover any ICT third-party service providers used.

As part of the ICT risk management framework, financial entities must set up communication
strategies4, policies and plans, at least for major ICT incidents and vulnerabilities, enabling a
responsible disclosure of incidents and vulnerabilities (Art. 14 of DORA). They are to
distinguish between the respective addressees, which explicitly include the general public.
Financial entities must task at least one person with implementing the communication
strategy for ICT-related incidents. This person will fulfil the public and media function for that
purpose (Art. 14(3) of DORA).

Financial entities may voluntarily participate in an exchange of information concerning cyber
threats and information within trusted communities (Art. 45(1) of DORA). Financial entities
must notify the supervisory authority of their participation in such information-sharing
arrangements and of the cessation of their cooperation (Art. 45(3) of DORA). Under Art. 49(1)
of DORA, supervisory authorities may offer crisis management and contingency exercises in
order to practise a coordinated response and to develop communication channels.



3. IT operations

This section compares the stipulations in Art. 7, 8, 9 and 12 of DORA and Art. 4, 5, 8, 9 and 17
of RTS RMF for the operation of IT systems with chapter 8, IT operations, of the BAIT/VAIT.

IT operations have gained in significance in the context of stronger digital operational
resilience. DORA includes more stringent requirements regarding up-to-date, reliable and
technologically resilient ICT systems. Moreover, the detailed overview of ICT systems is more
closely linked to the business functions. The ICT systems and information a nd their reciprocal
impacts are now jointly identified, classified and documented in the form of an inventory. In
addition, the volume of changes to these systems which must be taken into consideration
within the scope of change management is greater than before. The backup and recovery of
data are supplemented with requirements such as segregated systems and multiple checks
following a recovery.

3.1 Stronger operational stability

Operational stability and the updating of ICT systems is a further area of focus in the context
of digital operational resilience. This is emphasised in Art. 7 of DORA, which thus intensifies
the existing requirements in the BAIT/VAIT. For instance, Art. 7 of DORA requires “updated
ICT systems“ and accordingly exceeds the existing regulation, which mainly required
updating of IT systems (chapter 8.3 of the BAIT/VAIT). In addition, the requirements as to the
reliability (Art. 7(b) of DORA) and technological resilience (Art. 7(d) of DORA) of ICT systems
have been expanded. ICT systems must ensure adequate information processing even during
stressed market phases. The BAIT/VAIT do not emphasise these requirements as explicitly.

In addition, capacity management, which is familiar from chapter 8.8 of the BAIT/VAIT, is
expanded through Art. 7(c) of DORA in conjunction with Art. 9 of RTS RMF. For instance, this
requires a greater volume of documentation than previously. Furthermore, measures must be
determined for the optimisation of resources and resource shortages must be analysed and
prevented before they occur, by means of appropriate monitoring. Art. 12(4) of DORA also
requires redundant ICT capacities with resources, capabilities and functions which are
adequate to ensure business needs.

Checks for legacy ICT systems are now also more significant in the context of strengthening
resilience. These systems already required management (chapter 8.3 of the BAIT/VAIT).
However, in future they must be explicitly checked yearly and as of any change in the ICT risk
and assessed for their level of ICT risk (Art. 8(7) of DORA).



3.2 Classification of ICT systems and information

As described in section 2.1, ICT systems and information used in business functions must be
identified and classified. The reciprocal impacts of the ICT assets and their links to the
business functions must be taken into consideration (Art. 8(1) of DORA in conjunction with
Art. 4 of RTS RMF). This method combines the previously separate definition of an
information domain and the listing of IT system components (chapters 3 and 8 of the
BAIT/VAIT) and thus provides a holistic picture of ICT systems and information which was not
previously available in this form.



3.3 Expansion to include all changes to ICT systems

The risk-based approach to change management already familiar from chapters 8.4 and 8.5
of the BAIT/VAIT is required in an expanded form in DORA (Art. 9(4)(e), (f) of DORA in
conjunction with Art. 17 of RTS RMF). The previous assessment was limited to significant
changes. In future, this limitation will no longer apply. Under Art. 9(4)(e) of DORA, in future
any changes to ICT systems will be in the scope of ICT change management and must be
recorded, tested, assessed, approved, implemented and verified in a controlled manner. This
means that financial entities must now consider a significantly greater volume of changes
than previously.

In addition, Art. 17 of RTS RMF includes stipulations regarding the minimum contents of the
ICT change management process, such as check requirements, testing of these changes,

impact analyses or fallback solutions. This exceeds the scope of the existing requirements.
Section 5.3 provides further information on this point from the perspective of project
management.



3.4 Segregated data retention and reconciliation of data backups

In principle, the data backup topic should already be familiar from the data backup
procedures required in chapter 8.7 of the BAIT/VAIT. Art. 12 of DORA requires the definition
of a policy for backup procedures as well as restoration and recovery procedures and
methods. This expands the scope of the BAIT/VAIT requirements, which mainly required data
backup procedures.

DORA contains conceptual requirements which are analogous to the familiar data backup
procedures (Art. 12(1)(a) of DORA). Policies and procedures must be developed and
documented which ensure minimum downtime in the restoration of ICT systems and data as
well as limited disruption and loss.

In addition, the backup and restoration of data under Art. 12(2) of DORA requires financial
entities to ensure that the security of the network and information systems and the
availability, authenticity, integrity and confidentiality of data are not endangered. This
requirement is not included in the BAIT/VAIT, particularly in relation to the security of
systems (chapter 8.7 of the BAIT/VAIT).

The backup and restoration procedures must be regularly tested in accordance with Art. 12(2)
of DORA and chapter 8.7 of the BAIT/VAIT. In regard to the restoration of data, it must be
noted that Art. 12(3) of DORA prescribes ICT systems which are physically and logically
segregated from the source system. In the BAIT/VAIT, this is only listed as a possible
requirement. DORA therefore goes beyond the existing stipulations in the BAIT/VAIT with
regard to data retention.

Moreover, regarding data recovery following ICT-related incidents, Art. 12(7) of DORA
introduces data checks which include multiple checks and reconciliations in order to ensure
that the highest possible level of data integrity is maintained. The BAIT/VAIT do not require
data reconciliation and checks in this form.

The reporting of unscheduled deviations from standard operations (disruptions) is already
familiar from chapter 8.6 of the BAIT/VAIT. DORA uses the term “error” for these disruptions
(Art. 8(2)(c) of RTS RMF). These errors must continue to be assessed by means of appropriate
procedures and protocols, analogously to disruptions.

In addition, within the scope of ICT risk management, DORA makes reference to extensive
additional requirements relating to the treatment, classification and reporting of ICT-related
incidents (cf. Chapter III, Art. 17-23 of DORA) and testing (cf. Chapter IV, Art. 24-27 of DORA).
However, these guidance notes on implementation do not cover Chapters III and IV of DORA.

4. ICT business continuity management

This section compares the relevant ICT business continuity management/response and
recovery requirements (hereinafter: “ICT business continuity management”) in Art. 11, 12 and
14 of DORA and Art. 24 to 26 of RTS RMF with the IT operations and IT business continuity
management chapters of the BAIT/VAIT (chapter 10 in each case).

In summary, it should be noted that Art. 11 of DORA and Art. 24 to 26 of RTS RMF go beyond
the scope of the requirements in chapter 10 of the BAIT/VAIT. Key differences relate to
changes in the contents as well as the structure of the relevant policies and plans, with the
ICT business continuity policy at the heart of this.

In addition, the number of scenarios to be taken into consideration is greater than in the
BAIT/VAIT: For example, the impacts of climate change and insider attacks must be included,
particularly when developing ICT response and recovery plans.

Further changes relate to the review of ICT business continuity management. In DORA, the
management body is responsible for this with regard to the ICT business continuity policy
and the ICT response and recovery plans, while this is not specified as such in the BAIT/VAIT.
DORA prescribes periodic and at least annual intervals for the review and testing of the
various ICT business continuity management plans and thus differs from the BAIT/VAIT.
Moreover, through the establishment of a crisis management function and a stronger focus
on (crisis) communication in general, DORA makes these requirements significantly more
stringent in comparison to the BAIT/VAIT.

In addition, DORA requires reporting to the competent authorities upon their request
concerning the estimated aggregated annual costs and losses caused by major ICT-related
incidents. Relevant details are specified in guidelines. This reporting is not covered by these
guidance notes on implementation.



4.1 Revised structure and contents of policies and plans

As a central element of ICT business continuity management, Art. 11(1) of DORA introduces a
new ICT business continuity policy which is an integral part of the overall business continuity
policy and is based on identification requirements set out in Art. 8 of DORA (see section 2.2).
The BAIT/VAIT do not include the phrase “ICT business continuity policy”. They use the
phrase “business continuity plans” in their IT operations chapter and in the introduction to
the IT service continuity management chapter (BAIT).

The objectives of the ICT business continuity policy are listed in Art. 11(2) of DORA. As well as
the requirement for it to be documented, this policy includes, in particular, the following:
     an appropriate response to all ICT-related incidents for the purpose of damage
       limitation, combined with the resumption of activities and the prioritisation of
       recovery actions;
     the activation of plans with containment measures, processes and technologies for
       ICT-related incidents as well as




Guidance notes on the implementation of DORA                                          Page 16 of 39
       the mandatory estimation of preliminary impacts, damages and losses (as already
        implemented, where applicable, within the scope of PSD2 reports on major
        operational and security incidents);
       setting out communication and crisis management actions.

The components of the ICT business continuity policy are described in detail in Art. 24 of RTS
RMF. A business impact analysis (BIA) and risk analysis must be taken into consideration for
severe business disruptions when implementing the general business continuity policy. The
risk analysis in DORA forms part of the BIA (Art. 11(5) of DORA and chapters 10.3, 10.4 of the
VAIT). The relevant framework conditions must be specified in the business continuity policy.

In determining the recovery time and recovery point objectives for each function, beside s the
question of whether it is a critical or important function, financial entities are to take into
account the potential overall impact on market efficiency (Art. 12(6) of DORA and chapter
10.3/10.5 of the BAIT/VAIT). The latter is introduced in DORA.

The business continuity plans specified in chapters 8.7, 10.1/8.7 of the BAIT/VAIT are not
present in DORA in the same form. However, DORA does feature a similar construct in the
ICT business continuity plans (Art. 11(4) of DORA). These plans must be periodically
maintained (Art. 11(4) of DORA) and tested (see section 4.3). Art. 25 of RTS RMF prescribes
more far-reaching requirements for the testing of ICT business continuity plans. Inter alia,
outsourcing and contractual agreements with ICT third-party service providers must be
included as of the creation, updating and testing of business continuity plans, particularly
where this relates to critical or important functions (Art. 25(2) of RTS RMF). The findings of
the above-mentioned BIA and the ICT risk assessment must be taken into account
(Art. 3(1)(b) of DORA).

The term “IT (business) contingency/continuity plans“ relating to restart, emergency
operation and recovery plans (cf. in particular chapter 10.3/10.5 of the BAIT/VAIT) is not
found in DORA. DORA introduces ICT response and recovery plans at this level (Art. 11(3)
of DORA). These plans are specified in further detail in Art. 26 of RTS RMF.

As with the ICT business continuity plans, the ICT response and recovery plans must be tested
(Art. 11(6)(a) of DORA, see section 4.3). Art. 11(6)(b) of DORA likewise prescribes testing of
crisis communication plans. Such a requirement is not found in the BAIT/VAIT. In DORA, the
test findings are to be documented in writing and any deficiencies determined through the
tests are to be analysed, remedied and reported to the management body.



4.2 Expansion of mandatory scenarios

The mandatory inclusion of specific scenarios is specified in DORA during creation of the ICT
response and recovery plans (Art. 26(2) of RTS RMF) and linked with the components of the
ICT business continuity policy as well as the general business continuity policy
(Art. 24(1)(b)(i)(1) and Art. 25(2)(e) of RTS RMF). In addition, the number of scenarios which
must be taken into consideration differs by comparison with the BAIT/VAIT. The following
scenarios have been added:

       impact of climate change and events associated with environmental degradation,
        natural disasters, pandemics and physical attacks, including intrusions and terrorist
        attacks;
       insider attacks;
       political and social instability, including, where relevant, in the ICT third-party service
        provider’s jurisdiction and the location where the data are stored and processed and
       widespread power outages.



4.3 Regular review of ICT business continuity management

The BAIT require a regular review of whether the (IT) contingency plans is effective and
appropriate, while the VAIT require a regular review of whether the IT business continuity
concept (incl. the IT contingency plans) is up-to-date; the VAIT require updating of the IT
business continuity concept on an event-driven basis. In DORA, the management body or the
financial entity is responsible for the approval and periodic oversight of the ICT business
continuity policy and the ICT response and recovery plans (Art. 5(2)(e) of DORA and Art. 11(6)
of DORA in regard to the review). However, the differences in the terms used must be noted
here. It therefore not possible to easily draw a direct comparison.

DORA does not contain any direct equivalent of the requirement in the BAIT of an at least
annual review of the effectiveness and appropriateness of all relevant scenarios and activities.

The periodic testing of ICT business continuity plans which is required in Art. 11(6)(a) of
DORA does not have any direct equivalent in the BAIT/VAIT. The testing of the ICT response
and recovery plans which is likewise required is analogous to the requirements for the annual
(BAIT) or regular (VAIT) testing of the effectiveness of the IT contingency plans, since they
overlap with these IT contingency plans. However, this testing must be carried out at least
annually and in the event of any significant changes to ICT systems which support critical or
important functions (Art. 11(6)(a) of DORA).

As already discussed in section 2.2, the ICT risk management framework which includes ICT
business continuity management must be checked at least annually or at a similar interval.



4.4 Strengthening of crisis management and communication

Art. 11(7) of DORA requires the establishment of a crisis management function. As of the
activation of ICT business continuity plans or ICT response and recovery plans, inter alia this
function is to specify clear procedures for the handling of internal and external crisis
communication (Art. 14 of DORA).

Communication and crisis management actions must be established, inter alia within the
scope of the ICT business continuity policy and, in particular, in the context of ICT-related
incidents. The purpose of these measures is to notify external stakeholders as well as involved
internal staff (Art. 11(2)(e) in conjunction with Art. 14 and 19 of DORA; Art. 24(1)(a)(iv) and
(b)(vi) of RTS RMF). For the purpose of implementation, communication strategies and
policies must be developed which take into consideration the information requirements of

the employees involved in the response and recovery and additional staff that need to be
informed (Art. 14(2) of DORA).



5. ICT project management and application development

This section compares the stipulations in Art. 15, 16 and 17 of RTS RMF concerning ICT
project and change management with the IT project management and application
development requirements in chapter 7 of the BAIT/VAIT.

Overall, it should be noted that the individual requirements with a specific IT security slant in
RTS RMF are considerably more detailed than those in the chapter of the BAIT/VAIT on ICT
projects and application development. The RTS clearly focuses on the i mplementation of
suitable measures to fulfil the security goals and less on the governance of specific processes,
as in the BAIT/VAIT. The measures listed in RTS RMF are minimum requirements and should
not be considered exhaustive. Additional measures accompanying the minimum
requirements may also be required here, in line with the principle of proportionality.

Where the BAIT/VAIT have been implemented, the new regulatory system provides various
degrees of freedom in terms of the implementation of the requi rements. DORA specifies
fewer details and examples in relation to ICT project and change management. Instead, the
level of security is defined by means of minimum requirements, but there is greater freedom
as regards implementation. A moderate volume of additional work is nonetheless necessary,
e.g. for source code analysis and in order to consider the removal of the materiality threshold
during the implementation.



5.1 Analogous requirements for ICT project management

The basic requirements for an ICT project methodology (Art. 15 of RTS RMF) which must be
fulfilled by means of a corresponding policy are also found in RTS RMF (Art. 15(1)-(3) of RTS
RMF), analogously to chapter 7.3 of the BAIT/chapter 7.4 of the VAIT. Simplifications arise
where correlations across multiple projects need to be considered. Here, the BAIT/VAIT
currently prescribe that a risk assessment must be implemented in relation to the
interdependencies between different ICT projects. DORA no longer requires this assessment.
Instead, projects must be evaluated with a view to their impact on critical or important
functions and communicated to the management body in accordance with their importance
and the size of the project in question (Art. 15(5) of RTS RMF).

Further simplifications arise due to the removal of the requirements as to the qualitative and
quantitative resources and restrictions relating to the information security perspective, which
merely requires compliance with the security objectives (Art. 15(1) of RTS RMF). Nor does RTS
RMF include requirements relating to project documentation and lessons learned.




Guidance notes on the implementation of DORA                                          Page 19 of 39
5.2 Detailed stipulations regarding ICT system acquisition, development and
    maintenance

RTS RMF goes into significantly greater detail than the BAIT/VAIT and contains concrete
minimum elements for an acquisition, development and maintenance policy (Art. 16(1) of RTS
RMF). Unlike the BAIT/VAIT, the focus is less on documentation of application development
and instead more on secure implementation (Art. 16(1)(a) and (c) of RTS RMF) and
identification of the requirements necessary to implement the application (Art. 16(1)(b) of
RTS RMF). Full documentation of requirements, in the sense of a technical concept or
requirements specification document, is not explicitly stipulated.

RTS RMF describes in great detail the measures to be implemented for testing of IT systems
(Art. 16(2) of RTS RMF). For instance, in future security testing is required within the scope of
the source code review for internet-exposed systems and applications (Art. 16(3) of RTS
RMF).

Here, the VAIT merely require the introduction of a standard process, without indicating
concrete testing procedures or scenarios.

Unlike the BAIT/VAIT, there are no longer any requirements relating to stress scenarios or
documentation of testing. However, in case of changes, Art. 17 of RTS RMF requires an
effective testing procedure, including quality assurance (Art. 17(1)(c)(ii) of RTS RMF), which
does suggest documentation.

RTS RMF contains highly specific requirements in relation to the handling of source code
from application development. The source code must be checked for anomalies by means of
static and dynamic testing prior to productive use (Art. 16(3) of RTS RMF). This also applies
for source code created by third parties and for proprietary software (compiled source code)
(Art. 16(8) of RTS RMF).

End-user computing (EUC) is only implicitly included in RTS RMF. No distinction is made
between EUC and bought-in (standard) applications. Instead, it is pointed out that
developments outside of the IT function must be checked for risks (Art. 16(9) of RTS RMF).
The key requirements for EUC continue to apply. Testing may be more extensive than
previously due to the lack of a special status for EUC.



5.3 Removal of the materiality threshold for ICT change management

In the case of ICT change management (Art. 17 of RTS RMF), changes by comparison with the
existing BAIT/VAIT requirements relate, in particular, to the assessment of changes to
software, hardware, firmware, systems or security parameters (Art. 17(1) of RTS RMF). In
particular, there is a change of perspective here, since data as an asset worthy of protection
and the technology used are now the central point of focus (Art. 17(1) of RTS RMF). In
contrast, the BAIT/VAIT consider the effect of the changes in the context of the overall IT
organisation within the scope of an impact analysis. However, RTS RMF includes concrete
requirements for the operational process for the handling of changes (Art. 17(1) of RTS RMF).
It refers here, inter alia, to the separation of functions and roles (Art. 17(1)(b) of RTS RMF) as


Guidance notes on the implementation of DORA                                           Page 20 of 39
part of the change approval process. It does not describe or require the implementation of a
concrete process.

To date, chapter 7.1 of the BAIT/VAIT stipulated that significant changes must undergo a
prescribed analysis process and that, depending on the materiality of the changes, further
affected organisational units must be involved. RTS RMF no longer distinguishes between a
material and non-material change. Accordingly, in future all changes in ICT systems must be
recorded, tested, assessed, approved, implemented and verified in a controlled manner
(Art. 17(1)(c) of RTS RMF). RTS RMF does not explicitly require the involvement of additional
organisational units, which is stipulated in the BAIT/VAIT.



6. ICT third-party risk management

This section compares the ICT third-party risk management requirements set out in Art. 1, 5
and Chapter V Section I (Art. 28-30) of DORA, RTS TPPol and draft RTS SUB with those in
chapter 9 of the BAIT (“Outsourcing and other external procurement of IT services”) and
chapter 9 of the VAIT (“Outsourcing of IT services and other service relationships in the area
of IT services”). Excerpts of the Minimum Requirements for Risk Management
(Mindestanforderungen an das Risikomanagement – MaRisk, section AT 9) and the Minimum
Requirements under Supervisory Law on the System of Governance of Insurance
Undertakings (Mindestanforderungen an die Geschäftsorganisation von
Versicherungsunternehmen – MaGo, chapter 13) are also considered, since they are closely
linked with the BAIT and VAIT requirements.

Chapter V of DORA regulates the use of ICT services provided by ICT third-party service
providers. A series of key principles for sound management of ICT third-party risk are defined
for this purpose. These cover requirements for governance, the life cycle for obtaining ICT
services, handling of specific ICT third-party risks and the minimum contractual contents. A
list of these minimum contractual contents is attached as an annex to these guidance notes
on implementation.



6.1 Distinction vis-à-vis outsourcing

ICT third-party risk management according to DORA supplements the existing sectoral
provisions on outsourcing 5. Financial entities must thus continue to comply with the sectoral
outsourcing requirements, i.e. the statutory requirements and the prescriptions e.g. in the
MaRisk and MaGo remain applicable. They apply in parallel and on a complementary basis. It
must thus be assumed that in many cases a contractual agreement on the usage of ICT
services will also constitute outsourcing (and vice versa). Harmonisation of the requirements
for ICT third-party risk management under DORA and the sectoral outsourcing requirements
is a supervisory objective.

The definition of contractual arrangements for the use of ICT services (Art. 28(1)(a) in

to run their business operations”. This necessitates an assessment of all ICT-related third-
party services and, at the level of implementation, uncertainties may arise regarding the
correct classification. In addition, in many cases the scope of scenarios covered will likely be
broader.

The issue of whether the bought-in ICT service supports a critical or important function is
particularly significant for the scope of the requirements which must be fulfilled. In such
cases, the associated ICT third-party risks are assumed to be particularly significant. From a
methodological and substantive point of view, the assessment of whether functions are
“critical or important” is not identical with a materiality determination for outsourcing 6. The
former is implemented on the basis of the effects resulting from a disruption or failed
performance of the function in question (Art. 3(22) of DORA). Here too, the development of
suitable criteria for classification (Art. 3(2) of RTS TPPol) and the assessment of all of the
issues are necessary.

Equally, in the case of critical or important functions within the scope of due diligence, audit
or assessment results of the ICT third-party service provider must be used where appropriate,
even before a contract is concluded (Art. 6(3)(a) and (b) of RTS TPPol). Some third-party
service providers are likely to find it challenging to provide such audit or assessment reports.



6.5 Changed exit requirements

The requirements for exit strategies/plans for ICT services that support critical or important
functions have increased significantly. In particular, the expectations regarding the goals of
the exit strategies have expanded significantly (Art. 28(8) of DORA). Financial entities must

thus be able to exit contractual arrangements without disruption to their business activities
or detriment to services provided to clients and without limiting compliance with regulatory
requirements. The exit plans must be based on plausible scenarios and appropriate
assumptions (Art. 10 of RTS TPPol). In addition, these exit plans must be sufficiently tested
and are to be periodically reviewed.

The previous opening clause for outsourcing within a group or network of credit institutions
(non-requirement of exit processes under section AT 9 chapter 15 (d) of the MaRisk) does not
apply if the services in question are ICT services. However, it remains possible to take
proportionality into consideration in the context of a reduced level of risk (where applicable)
(Art. 1(e) of RTS TPPol 11). This also relates to ICT third-party service providers which are
themselves supervised or monitored (Art. 1(g) of RTS TPPol).

Under DORA, concentration risks are considered extensively and highly specifically, with the
goal of identifying them and appropriately monitoring them. For this purpose, for contractual
arrangements relating to critical or important functions, financial entities are to identify and
assess whether the ICT third-party service provider would not be readily replaceable or
whether multiple ICT services are procured from a given ICT third-party service provider.
Where concentration risks are applicable, financial entities must weigh the benefits and costs
of alternative solutions (Art. 29(1) of DORA). This concentration risk is included in the ex ante
risk analysis under Art. 5 of RTS TPPol and in the analyses for subcontracting under
Art. 3(1)(h) of draft RTS SUB.



6.6 Changes in governance of ICT third-party risk

Changes have arisen in the area of governance. In particular, the management body is
involved to a greater extent, e.g. through the review of ICT third-party risks (Art. 28(2) of
DORA) or the approval of the policy for the use of ICT services which support critical or
important functions (Art. 5(2)(h) of DORA and Art. 3(1) of RTS TPPol), as well as reporting
channels for the use of ICT services (Art. 5(2)(i) of DORA). Financial entities must establish a
role in order to monitor the arrangements concluded with ICT third-party service providers
on the use of ICT services (Art. 5(3) of DORA, see also section 1.2), which is analogous to the
(central) outsourcing manager.



6.7 Notice on reporting obligations and information register

However, these guidance notes on implementation do not cover reporting obligations,
particularly in relation to the information register, the annual report to the competent
authorities and the reporting of planned contractual arrangements (Art. 28(3) of DORA).

7. Operational information security

This section compares the requirements for data and system security measures set out in
Art. 9 and 10 of DORA and Art. 6, 7, 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 22 and 23 of RTS RMF with those on
operational information security in chapter 5 of the BAIT/VAIT.

In general, on the topic of operational information security in DORA, it must be noted that
the requirements are specified in significantly greater detail than in chapter 5 of the
BAIT/VAIT. The level of detail is similar to the explanatory notes provided in the BAIT/VAIT for
the minimum requirements for appropriate information security measures and processes.

Art. 11 of RTS RMF requires data and system security measures which ensure that:

       hardening measures are implemented and periodically reviewed,
       software and mobile data storage media are only used following authorisation (white-
        list approach),
       teleworking arrangements are in place which cover permitted devices and software,
       processes are available for the secure deletion of information and the destruction of
        data storage media and
       measures have been implemented to protect against the intentional or inadvertent
        loss or leak of data.

In addition, more stringent requirements apply for ICT systems operated by ICT third-party
service providers. These include:

       the institution implementing manufacturers’ recommendations for the ICT systems
        which it operates,
       clearly setting out information security roles and responsibilities between financial
        entities and ICT third-party service providers,
       the financial entity retaining sufficient capabilities in order to manage and ensure the
        security of the ICT solution used and
       technical and organisational measures having been implemented in order to reduce
        the risks for the financial entity’s own infrastructure which arise from the use of the
        ICT third-party service provider.



7.1 Stronger network security

Art. 13 of RTS RMF stipulates network security requirements which are already familiar from
chapters 4.3 and 5.2 of the BAIT/VAIT. In addition, there are detailed requirements e.g.
relating to network segmentation and segregation, network access control and third-party
device recognition, hardening of all network components and network architecture reviews. A
greater volume of work is expected to arise in the implementation of the requirement in
Art. 9(4)(b) of DORA for the automated isolation of information assets in the event of cyber-
attacks. Further detail is provided on this point in Art. 13(1)(j) of RTS RMF.

With the increasing stringency of network security requirements, the role of firewalls is also
more strongly emphasised. For firewall rules, a life cycle including defined roles and
responsibilities must be implemented and firewall recertification must thus be specified

Guidance notes on the implementation of DORA                                          Page 26 of 39
(Art. 13(1)(h) of RTS RMF). Firewall rules which support critical or important functions in ICT
systems must be recertified at minimum on a semi-annual basis, analogously to the
provisions for access management.

The protection of information while in transit is particularly emphasised in Art. 14 of RTS RMF.
However, this does not stipulate any specific requirements and instead requires policies and
procedures which ensure protection while taking the risk profile into consideration (Art. 14(2)
of RTS RMF). Another new element is the requirement in Art. 14(1)(c) of RTS RMF that
confidentiality or non-disclosure arrangements for risks which cannot be technically
mitigated must be concluded with staff of the financial entity and with the affected staff of
the ICT third-party service providers.



7.2 Encryption of data even while in use

Art. 9 of DORA and Art. 6 and 7 of RTS RMF stipulate significantly more stringent
requirements for the encryption of information than in chapter 5.2 of the BAIT/VAIT. Data
must be encrypted in line with its level of criticality in any situation (at rest, in transit and in
use). While chapter 5.2 of the BAIT/VAIT stipulates requirements regarding the encryption of
stored data and during transfer in line with the protection requirements, the requirement in
Art. 9(2) of DORA for encryption of data while “in use” is a new aspect whose implementation
is likely to require a significant amount of work. These requirements specify that in those
cases where encryption is not possible while data is in use, data must be processed in
separate, specially protected environments or else other suitable measures must be
implemented.

Another new requirement is that cryptographic keys, which were not directly covered in the
BAIT/VAIT, are now specifically dealt with in Art. 7 of RTS RMF. A policy and a procedure must
be established for handling cryptographic keys along with measures for their protection. The
entire life cycle, from generation to destruction, must be taken into consideration. Should
keys be lost, damaged or compromised, financial entities must ensure that they can be
replaced. Under Art. 7(4) of RTS RMF, at a minimum for those ICT assets which support critical
or important functions, a register of all certificates and certificate-storing devices must be
kept which is up-to-date at all times.



7.3 Timely identification and handling of vulnerabilities

Art. 10 of RTS RMF sets out how financial entities are to identify, deal with and handle
vulnerabilities in a timely fashion. Financial entities must thus ensure that

       ICT third-party service providers handle vulnerabilities which relate to the ICT systems
        provided to the financial entity in question and report them to this financial entity in a
        timely manner in the event of critical vulnerabilities and statistics and trends,
       vulnerabilities are also responsibly and suitably communicated to external parties and,
        where appropriate, to the general public,




Guidance notes on the implementation of DORA                                            Page 27 of 39
       automated vulnerability scans are implemented for the purpose of timely
        identification, with an at least weekly vulnerability scan being required for ICT systems
        which support critical or important functions,
       externally procured and open-source software libraries are likewise included in the
        vulnerability process.

Vulnerabilities must be dealt with according to priority levels drawn up by the financial entity
(Art. 10(2)(f) of RTS RMF), taking into consideration the criticality of the vulnerability, the
classification of the asset and the risk profile. The patching of vulnerabilities must be
prioritised over other measures. Where possible, available software and hardware patches
and updates must be identified and evaluated by means of automated tools (Art. 10(4)(a) of
RTS RMF). A separate procedure must be established for the installation of patches in case of
“emergencies” (Art. 10(4)(b) of RTS RMF). If patches are not installed within the prescribed
time limits, a defined escalation procedure must be initiated (Art. 10(4)(d) of RTS RMF).

In DORA, the threats to the information domain which are familiar from Art. 5.3 of the
BAIT/VAIT are not identified by means of “potentially security-related information” but
instead via “anomalous activities” (Art. 10 of DORA).

Using appropriate procedures, anomalous activities and behaviour must be detected for this
purpose (Art. 10(1) of DORA); early warning indicators (Art. 17(3)(a) of DORA) must be
identified and all detection mechanisms must be regularly tested (Art. 25 of DORA); and
detected ICT-related incidents are to be handled within the expected resolution time, both
during and outside normal working hours (Art. 23(2)(c) of RTS RMF). Sufficient technical and
organisational resources are to be made available for this purpose.

For the sake of completeness, it should be mentioned that, within the scope of ICT risk
management, DORA makes reference to extensive additional requirements relating to the
treatment, classification and reporting of ICT-related incidents (cf. Chapter III, Art. 17-23 of
DORA) and testing (cf. Chapter IV, Art. 24-27 of DORA). However, these guidance notes on
implementation do not cover Chapters III and IV of DORA. It is evident, though, that the
identification of anomalous behaviour in particular exceeds the scope of the strong use case-
driven approach in chapter 5.4 of the BAIT/VAIT.

Art. 12 of RTS RMF stipulates extensive logging requirements by way of a basis for
identification of anomalous activities.

           The retention period for logs is based on the business and security requirements,
            the purpose of retention and the risk profile of the affected ICT assets. It is
            important that these decisions are sufficiently justified and documented.
           Logs must be protected against tampering and deletion. Financial entities must
            monitor any outage in the logging system and detect outages.
           All of the financial entity’s ICT systems must be synchronised with a reliable
            reference time.

8. Identity and access management

This section compares the stipulations relating to access management in Art. 18, 20 and 21 of
RTS RMF with those in chapter 6 of the BAIT/VAIT.

For identity and access management, it should be noted that DORA does not entail all that
many changes by comparison with the BAIT/VAIT. Familiar processes such as the request,
assignment and recertification processes remain as described in chapter 6 of the BAIT/VAIT.
In terms of identity and access management, the existing requirements are actually more
detailed than those set out in Chapter II of RTS RMF.



8.1 Explicit identity management requirements

Art. 20 of RTS RMF stipulates concrete identity management requirements. This was not
explicitly required in the BAIT/VAIT, but they already set out identity management
requirements as a basis for access management. The effort required to adjust existing
processes is likely to be limited. Under Art. 20 of RTS RMF, identity management guidelines
and procedures must be developed, documented and implemented. These guidelines must
stipulate that

       all employees (including employees of ICT third-party service providers) who access
        information assets and ICT assets of the financial entity are each assigned a unique
        identity,
       they retain these assigned unique identities following any reorganisation a nd upon
        expiry of the contractual relationship and
       a lifecycle management process is introduced for identities and accounts, with
        automated solutions ideally being implemented.



8.2 Introduction of the “need-to-use” principle

Access management (Art. 21 of RTS RMF) adds the “need-to-use” principle to the “need-to-
know” and “least privilege” principles from chapter 6.2 of the BAIT/VAIT. However, the new
principle mirrors the “need-to-know” and “least privilege” principles in chapter 6.2 of the
BAIT/VAIT, so that this is not expected to require a significant volume of work. Other
requirements include

       ensuring a separation of functions,
       largely limiting generic accounts so that activities can always be clearly allocated to a
        specific person and
       introducing controls in order to prevent unauthorised access.

One new aspect relates to the recertification of access rights. This is to be implemented every
six months for all authorisations relating to critical or important functions (Art. 21(1)(e)(iv) of
RTS RMF). For all other authorisations, annual recertification is sufficient. It is not necessary to
distinguish here between end user access and technical access.




Guidance notes on the implementation of DORA                                            Page 29 of 39
Privileged emergency access or administrative access may only be granted on a “need -to-
use” and ad-hoc basis. Where possible, automated solutions are to be used for privileged
access management (PAM). Privileged and remote access require strong authentication
(based on leading practices) (Art. 21(1)(f)(ii) of RTS RMF).
